From Achen’s Desk 

Dear Brothers and Sisters,

Grace and peace to you in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. I thank you for your encouragement and prayer. As we step into the month of August, we are approaching the season of harvest. I invite you to prepare your hearts and homes for our Harvest Festival, a cherished time in the life of our church. The Harvest Festival is more than just a celebration of crops gathered. It is a moment of deep thanksgiving to God, who provides for our needs in every season. It is a time to pause, reflect, and recognize that all we have, comes from the hands of God: the food we eat, the work we do, the health we enjoy, and the love we share in our families and community.

August is also the time of the year when we uphold a vital part of our parish life: the fellowship of the laity—the community of believers who, by baptism, are called to live and share the Gospel in everyday life. In its deepest identity, Church is a community of the faithful—lay men and women—who form the Body of Christ. The laity is the Church in the world. Each and every one of us carry the message of Christ into our homes, workplaces, schools, and neighborhoods. Our witness, our presence, and our prayers are essential to the life and mission of the Church.

Fellowship among the laity is not merely social—it is spiritual, missional, and communal. When believers come together in prayer, service, and encouragement, the Church becomes stronger. In Acts 2:42, we read: "They devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching and to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer."

This fellowship was not just about gathering; it was about building one another up in faith, standing together in times of struggle, and rejoicing together in times of blessing. I urge each of you to stay connected. In a world that often isolates and divides, Christian fellowship unites, heals, and strengthens. It reminds us that we are never alone in our walk with Christ.

Finally, I invite you to continue praying for peace in our world and for those in our community who are ill, grieving, or in need. May our parish always be a place of compassion, hospitality, and hope.

Rev. Sandeep Jacob
